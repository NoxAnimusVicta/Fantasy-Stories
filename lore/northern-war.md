# The northern dominion and the eight-year war

The Winter Seat occupies the moving ice. The King's older conquests lie along its southern rim, where exposed rock carries roads, mines, burial halls and the ruins of hill kingdoms. In his dominion a dead mason can spend an entire winter dressing stone under a foreman's orders. Across centuries the King used such labour to join isolated conquests by roads wide enough for whole columns. He cut seals in burial grounds he had personally taken. A captain stationed there could receive new bodies long after the King had gone elsewhere.

## Why Vardane fell within reach

Between the ice and the Vardanian lakes lies the Narrows, a long defile between a sheer ridge and deep water. Three northern fortresses controlled its crossings. Their lords could bar the only all-season road south. Elsewhere the King could send raiding columns across winter ice, but the spring thaw stranded them from his siege train and the roads back to his seals. The Winter Seat takes weeks to move on glacial ice. It could never follow a raiding party onto the lake shore.

The last northern fortress fell shortly before the invasion. Its capture gave the King the third crossing, the roads onward and stone quays from which his captains could commandeer lake craft. He had spent centuries acquiring a dominion. With the road through the Narrows in his hands, he ordered its armies into Vardane the following spring.

## The campaigns

| Campaign years | What happened | What survived it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First | The dead descended the Narrows and took the upper lake ports. The King visited the captured shore and sealed the great vault at Dunmere. | Vardane lost its northern road. Dunmere became the first dependable place to raise replacements inside the kingdom. |
| Second and third | Mara evacuated the lake basin. Requisitioned boats kept several island castles supplied after the mainland roads fell. Cyrannese regiments joined her on the middle river. | River crossings and fortified islands divided the host into columns which the living could sometimes defeat separately. |
| Fourth | The allies retook Dunmere by water. A dwarven company broke the sealed vault stone before the relief force withdrew. | The King kept the northern ports. His eastern columns had to haul their dead back through the Narrows until he returned to establish another seal. The survivors won a full campaigning summer on that shore. |
| Fifth through seventh | The King revisited the eastern shore, reclaimed Dunmere and established several raising sites. His captains spent successive winters taking the causeways between the middle lakes. | The living retained the chain of river forts and the navigable water between them. Mara moved her court for the second time. |
| Eighth, the present | The last causeway fell before thaw. For the first time the host can move between the lakes on firm ground throughout the year, bypassing forts built to control the water. | Towns are falling in rapid succession along the opened roads. Several forts upstream still hold. Their crews can see enemy wagons using roads beyond bowshot. |

The losses reported across the current offensive belong to a front with many moving columns. A silent town may be evacuated, isolated or occupied. A local victory can preserve a district or destroy a raising site. No campaign in this history has destroyed the King's body.
