# The Inland Sun

Below eastern Kharad there is a country called Orr. Its sun is level with the streets. A traveller entering by the upper stair sees the roofs of the city in darkness and the undersides of its bridges bright as noon. Farther down, the light comes through a gate of horn. The gate opens and shuts of its own accord. It has lashes longer than the bridge.

## The birth of distance

In the oldest Orray hymn the whole of creation fits inside a woman's shut fist. Her name is Uru. She has held it shut so long that her nails have grown through her palm and become the five peoples. Each knows the others by the part that hurts when they move.

One of them wants to see his sister. He has slept against her all his life. He has felt her grow teeth. He wants to know what colour she is. He works his head free between two knuckles and opens his eye.

He sees her a mile away.

The hymn spends the next forty verses getting him back to her. He has to invent walking. She has to invent calling to someone too far away to touch. By the time he reaches her they are both old. She tells him the colour of his eye and he dies pleased. Uru lays his head under Kharad. She leaves the eye open. The Orray sing that everything visible is still as far from its beloved as that first man wished his sister to be.

Their sculptors give Uru six fingers. A child asking who came from the sixth is traditionally allowed to put a hand inside the hollow statue. There is another hand in there. It takes some children a long time to pull free.

## The other shore

There are people on the far side of the eye. They call the illuminated surface on which Orr stands the White of the Prisoner. Their ships can be seen crossing the pupil at dusk. When one draws close, the people along the quay at Orr can see its passengers looking up at them through deep water. A passenger sees a crowd gathered at the bottom of the sea.

For almost four centuries the two shores communicated by painting signs large enough to see across the pupil. The first complete sentence the Orray received is carved above their quay. A schoolchild learns it as *Sister, I came back.* The people who painted it used the same sign for a sister and for the woman charged with identifying a body. The second sentence asked the Orray to turn the head over. They have sent that request every year since.

Orr once tried. The western quarter of the city stood up on end. The bronze rings still set into the eastern cliff are the moorings of three ships that fell through. The descendants of their crews own the highest houses in Orr. They hang their washing from the rings.

## The eighth year

Refugees from the northern war sleep on the quay now. Some have discovered they can see their abandoned houses in the pupil after the ships have gone. The first woman to recognise her kitchen watched a dead soldier come in and spend half the morning trying to coax her cat out from under the stove. She went back the following day with a saucer of milk. It is still against the horn, among several hundred other things people have brought for those rooms.

Nine days ago a ship came up under the quay carrying a head on its deck. Even at that distance the refugees could see snow caught in the lashes. The people aboard painted a sentence the interpreters have found quite easy to read.

*We turned ours.*
