# Armies of the living and the dead

## The allied muster

| Force | Who commands | What it brings to the war |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vardanian host | Queen Mara assigns commands. Lords bring household troops and the levies their estates owe. Chartered towns retain their own militia officers. | Knights, local guides, garrisons and boat crews who know the river crossings in flood and frost. Levies must eventually return to their districts. |
| Cyrannese northern regiments | Royal officers answer to Edren and serve under agreed Vardanian district commands. | Drilled foot and cavalry that can campaign beyond one campaigning season. Their officers need new appropriations to replace losses. |
| Kharadi companies | Each hold sends sworn companies under clan officers. The assembly coordinates use of the high roads. | Mailed foot, crossbows, masons and engineers. A hold can send a relief column while retaining the crews responsible for its own gates. |
| Other contingents | An elven house, halfling moot or orc chief commits the people bound to that authority. | Marchwardens bring knowledge of the woodland approaches. Village scouts guide troops between roads. A mounted orc warband can range far from a marching column. Their presence depends on a real oath or agreement. |

A general receiving reinforcements needs to know whose orders they will take and when their service ends. A guildmaster may promise teams to search a ruined district. Doing so gives the general neither the keys to the guildhouse nor command of every member wearing its crest.

## The host

The Crown carries the King's will. Dead captains retain judgment enough to command detached forces for a season. Under them, experienced officers relay orders to groups of ordinary dead. A rank can hold a door through a night of bitter cold. A change in the orders that requires judgment goes to an officer. Captains possess the military knowledge and memories the King can use. His command remains absolute.

Old skeletons form much of the marching foot. Newly raised regiments retain better equipment and sounder bodies. Dead cavalry needs serviceable mounts. The Scythe can raise beasts in the King's presence or at his seals under the same conditions as other remains. Draft teams and wagon wheels wear out even when drivers need no rest. A road littered with broken axles can stall the King's siege engines while his foot continues ahead.

The King's siege masters direct dead work crews and maintain engines taken from the north. Captured craftsmen who remember their trades are valuable among his officers. A raised mage retains knowledge under the rules in [Magic and faith](magic-and-faith.md). The King alone cuts a seal and calls up the bodies at it.

## What victory buys

Killing a captain deprives a column of its local judgment. Troops already ordered to hold a bridge continue holding it until another command reaches them. Recovering the wounded and dead after a field victory denies the enemy replacements. Breaking a seal closes that particular raising site while the armies it already supplied remain in the field. These are three distinct victories, each with a consequence that can endure into the following campaign.
