# Dwarves

Dwarves are stocky, long-lived and prodigiously strong through the back and shoulders. They cut their great holds into mountain spurs above the snowmelt. From outside, a visitor may see only a bridge, a pair of bronze doors and a ribbon of chimney smoke. The oldest halls lie deep enough that generations have eaten, married and grown old beneath the same carved ceiling.

## Hold, clan and craft

A hold belongs to its clans. Each has hearths, burial galleries and rights to particular seams, forges or stretches of worked stone. The ruler inherits the high seat within a royal clan. The other clans witness the accession and swear their companies to the new sovereign. A ruler who orders a gallery opened must still find a master willing to put their maker's mark over the arch. Should it fail, every dwarf who passes the ruins will know whose judgment failed with it.

Children take the clan name and learn the household craft. A gifted child may apprentice to another clan, an arrangement celebrated almost as lavishly as a marriage. A master presents an apprentice with a first good tool and expects to see it again when the apprentice produces work worthy of a personal mark. Dwarves abroad recognise one another's teachers by the shape of a hammer head.

## The ancestors and the mountain

The honoured dead lie in family galleries with their names cut at hand height so descendants can run their fingers through the letters. Dwarves swear serious oaths before these walls. A grievance may survive centuries, but so can a kindness. A human mason who once saved a dwarven crew might leave grandchildren who have only to give their name at a mountain gate to be fed and housed.

Dwarven stonework provides ventilation, drainage and clean water for whole underground towns. Its beauty is easiest to see in inhabited rooms. A nursery wall carries reliefs of badgers small enough for a child to trace. Polished copper mirrors carry daylight down to a terrace where old dwarves sit to read. Above ground, herd settlements and terraced fields provide much of a hold's food. A shut gate can save the halls while abandoning the people who feed them.

## The shield companies

Clans muster in mail beneath square standards. Shield companies defend stairs and passages they know from childhood. Crossbow companies, engineers and armoured axe-bearing veterans give a hold teeth beyond its gates. A relief column from the Holds of Kharad once wintered in Vardane after driving orc raiders out of the eastern passes. Its descendants still claim the right to quarter there under the original treaty. Human villages remember the winter bill.

Against the undead, dwarves can yield a gallery and wall it off. The hard decision is which side of that wall still contains living kin. Some holds have started keeping their masons at the outer gate during refugee admissions. Everyone who enters sees the cut stone stacked there and understands how quickly the gate can be sealed.
