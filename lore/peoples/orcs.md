# Orcs

Orcs are a tusked, broad-built people who travel in nomadic clans. A clan drives its herds between summer grazing and sheltered winter grounds. Its chief rules by strength. Its neighbours know its banner, the sound of its drums and the price of letting it cross their land unopposed.

## The chief and the challenge

The chief chooses when the clan moves, where it makes war and how the spoils are divided. Any adult of the clan can challenge for leadership. The challenger stakes a weapon upright before the chief's tent and waits beside it. When the clan gathers, the chief takes up a weapon and meets the challenger. The victor can accept submission or finish the fight. A chief who refuses to come out has yielded the clan.

A chief's children grow up with fine weapons, seasoned teachers and relatives eager to see them rule. They still have to win. If a chief dies on the march, contenders fight at the next full camp. Until then the household heads keep the wagons moving and the warband leaders command their own riders. A beaten chief who survives can expect to spend the rest of that season hearing younger orcs retell the fight.

## Clan country

An orc can name land his clan has claimed for generations without ever having slept under a permanent roof. The claims follow water, grass and weather. A clan may fiercely defend a pass it uses for only ten days each spring. A human lord who builds a watchtower there in autumn can spend a peaceful winter collecting tolls before the owners come back.

Households travel with heavy hide tents, wagons, cattle and remounts. They return to winter camps where stone windbreaks, corrals and forge hearths can serve generations. The smith brings the bellows and tools back each year. Deep snow can keep a clan in one valley for months. Winter is when mail gets mended, hides get worked and old grievances have time to ripen. In spring the clan splits into smaller travelling bands as the herds spread over fresh grass.

## Blood, spoils and the ancestors

Several generations share a household. Relations rear children together and answer for injuries done by their kin. Young orcs learn to ride with the herd and wrestle at camp. Their first place in a warband comes when a veteran will take them along and put a spare mount or a good weapon in their keeping. A young warrior who loses either has to face the orc who lent it.

Captured herds, iron, weapons and tribute sustain powerful clans. At the division of spoils a chief takes the first share and rewards the warriors who earned distinction. The whole camp watches. A chief who claims the best horses for idle kin will hear exactly who took those horses the next time a challenger stands outside the tent. Raided settlements remember burned steads and stolen cattle long after the clan has moved on.

Clan shamans tend the ancestral fires, name the dead and speak the pedigrees of chiefs. Tusks taken from honoured dead pass to their kin. Some families bind generations of them to a war standard. At a funeral the shaman recounts the deed by which the dead orc wanted to be remembered. Anyone who rode beside them may speak. A boast that survived a warrior's lifetime can come apart at the funeral feast.

## Warbands and hordes

A clan can send its warriors out while the households move on under guard. Wolf riders serve as scouts and raiders. On foot, orcs favour heavy shields, axes and broad-headed spears. Each warband fights beneath a familiar standard and knows the voices of its own leaders. A chief who promises a season's plunder can draw veterans from smaller clans to ride with the host.

A horde forms when one warlord brings several chiefs to heel or wins their allegiance through conquest and the promise of greater spoils. Each chief commands their clan beneath the warlord's banner. An oath given by one chief binds the people who follow that chief. A foreign king seeking passage across three clans' ranges may have to deal with all three. The death of a great warlord can leave a horde with several ambitious claimants and an army already in the field.

## The undead war

Orc mobility can carry a clan away from an advancing host while the ground is open. A herd snowbound in its winter valley has fewer choices. An undead army occupying the spring pass can threaten the clan's whole year without chasing a single rider. The Lich King's ability to raise sound remains also makes an abandoned field of orc dead a prize for his army.

Individual clans can fight the host, sell their strength to a threatened kingdom or raid a neighbour whose soldiers have gone north. Their existing feuds, grazing claims and sworn alliances determine which banners ride together. Specific clans, occupied ranges and wartime allegiances remain open.
