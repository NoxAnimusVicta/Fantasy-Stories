# Elves

Elves live for centuries. Their forest realms take shape on the same scale. A young elf plants beeches along a bridal path expecting to walk beneath their mature crowns. An elder can remember the river before it changed course and will still use the old ford's name when giving directions. Human maps of elven country consequently show roads that seem to end in water and empty hills bearing the names of royal halls.

## Woodland courts

An elven sovereign keeps a court among several great houses. Each house tends a particular woodland, watches its paths and answers the sovereign's summons. The long reign of a capable queen gives a realm extraordinary continuity. An incapable one can outlive generations of human neighbours while her rivals grow old waiting for her to make a mistake.

Their halls stand on stone footings among living trees. Roofs are steep enough to shed leaves and broad enough to shelter open galleries in the rain. Foresters train trees around bridges and along stream banks over decades. Beyond the halls lie managed groves, orchards, clearings and meadows. An outsider looking across an unbroken canopy can badly underestimate how many elves live beneath it.

## Memory, worship and kin

Elven families have few children. A whole house may celebrate a birth, and several generations can have a hand in raising the child. Young adults commonly spend years travelling or serving as marchwardens before taking on a house duty. An elf who stays abroad a little longer than intended can return to find that the human friend who saw them off has died of old age.

They honour powers of wood, running water and the turning heavens. Sacred groves have appointed singers who know the old names and histories of their trees. Timber for ships and houses comes from managed stands. A human ruler granted felling rights on one bank of a brook can provoke a war by sending cutters across it. Some elders still remember personally granting old treaties and find themselves arguing with descendants who possess quite different written terms.

## The marches

Elven archers and scouts excel in broken woodland. Households also maintain mailed spearmen to hold bridges, protect bowmen and defend the approaches to inhabited groves. Woodland magic is cultivated over time. A marchwarden can draw on workings maintained along their own boundary for generations. A company sent to fight on an unfamiliar plain must rely more heavily on its training.

The undead war divides the courts over where to commit their small armies. Some elders would fight beside Vardane while human forts still command the northern roads. Others remember lending troops to an earlier human king whose heirs later cleared the very woodland the treaty guaranteed. Both factions can name the elves who died fulfilling that old bargain. Those names carry weight in a room where their parents still sit.
