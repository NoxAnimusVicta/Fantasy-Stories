# The lake road at the Reed muster

Author's disposition for the first district muster after the last causeway fell, in year eight. This is a fixed military reference event. If play begins later, its reports have aged and events since the muster must be accounted for. The harvest ledger at Crow Lodge belongs to its own season.

Measure north along the west-bank road from Gannet. Reed Tower stands at 16 km. At 22 km a three-kilometre side lane reaches the hamlet of Byre End. At 26 km the lake freight road forks away from the river. At 31 km stands the abandoned parish quay. The nearest organised enemy foot hold the fork, **ten road kilometres beyond Reed and twenty-six from Gannet**. Reed and Gannet remain in living hands. There is no continuous line across the fields.

| Place at muster dawn | Actual position | Evidence available in Gannet |
|---|---|---|
| Reed Tower | Twenty-seven living defenders, including four boat hands. Their landing and tower well still work. They cannot prevent wagons passing on the inland road. | Yesterday's runner saw Mara's colours. He left before the night's boat arrived. |
| Byre End | The villagers left by the reed channel two nights ago. A lame cow and a tied church door are all that remain. Twelve evacuees reached Reed by boat in the night. Others dispersed to kin. | No roll of survivors has reached the bridge. The evacuated families know different parts of the list. |
| Freight fork | Captain Iven, once the lake court's receiver of tolls, holds the old weighing house with about ninety dead foot. His orders are to keep the eleven wagons moving south along the inland branch to the next lake depot. They carry wheel timber, iron fittings and stores. | Two burners saw the wagons. Neither saw inside them. Vann has no reliable count of the foot. |
| Parish quay | The dead took the landing and its houses three days before muster. The ferryman's household was killed. Its skiff, split when the loaded boats shoved off, lies on the slip. | A boatwoman at Reed saw the bodies at the slip. The Gannet register still lists the family as missing. |
| Upper wool wash, 4 km inland from Reed | Seven living households remain behind a flooded mill leat. Their last messenger is at Reed with a broken leg. They have neither boats nor access to the military road. | The district clerk has put a cross beside the place for want of an answer. |

Iven's command belongs to the lake offensive. He remembers the toll exemptions his daughter used to copy out for him and can still recognise his own household device. He answers wholly to the Crown. His transport depends on serviceable teams and wheels. A missed consignment delays the lake depot. It gives Reed time to bring people across the water. The inhabitants killed at the quay remain dead. Raising replacements requires access to the King's surviving sites farther north. No seal has been cut in this district.

Reed's commander has requested boats for the wool wash. Vann's warrant reaches Reed. An order granting boats at Gannet must still reach an owner and make the journey upriver. The district cannot claim that those seven households were saved merely because somebody signed the order.
