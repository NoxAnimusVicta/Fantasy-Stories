# Gannet and the western passes
Regional gazetteer • Eighth campaign year

## The country and its roads

The river at Gannet rises beneath Kharad's western escarpment, flows west through Gannet Bridge, then bends south past Tallowmarket into Vardane's middle river country. At Gannet the north road comes down the west bank from the lake forts. A company withdrawing from the lakes turns east here to get home. A family fleeing south can stay on the west bank all the way to market. Closing the bridge catches these people differently.

A litter party works in relays and takes about twice the ordinary foot time on the bench trail. The ford at Lime End crosses a feeder stream to the hay fields. It offers no way around Gannet Bridge.

**Tallowmarket** smells of rendered sheep fat. The chandlers buy carcasses the wool merchants barely look at. Its barges unload by a public crane. The town militia controls the quay and a merchant owns the crane chain. Either can stop a shipment. Flour and salt move uphill, wool, hides and worked iron come down. Since the causeways fell, a family can sell its winter blankets in the morning and find them on a factor's cart going north before dinner.

**Gannet Bridge** has three stone arches and an iron basket hung from the tollhouse for petitions. It hangs high enough that a child needs somebody to lift them. Behind the tollhouse is the Bill House. Villagers still bring quartering claims left unpaid since their grandfathers fed a dwarven relief company. Keld remembers the campaign as the winter it saved those villages. Each autumn its veterans march across the bridge under square banners. The shutters on the Vardanian side stay closed until they pass.

**Lime End** has the last wagon yard for Crow Lodge. Kiln crews have stripped the near hillside of decent firewood. At Seed Shelf, beside the lodge, an old sledge road from the hay terraces admits short farm wagons in dry autumn weather. It is an isolated local track. The shelves grow oats and hay. Autumn wagon grain for the lodge comes from these terraces. Grain arriving from Tallowmarket changes to packs at Lime End. This distinction matters when a requisition empties the local barns.

**Crow Lodge** has twelve winter residents, a bake oven, a roofed spring and a pair of doors wide enough to bring in a loaded litter. Relatives of the dead scratch names on the underside of the long table. When it fills they plane it smooth, burn the shavings and begin again. The keeper still charges for supper at that table. The guild pays for casualties who arrive penniless.

**Keld Gate and Red Well** depend on the same ten days of grass at the saddle. The dwarves turn dairy cattle up from their terraces. The orcs coming west from Red Well bring mares and calves. Years ago both sides could wait their turn. Since the royal host began buying hay, neither can afford to keep stock below for long. At the saddle stands a stone ram without its horns. Dwarves say an orc broke them off. The orcs still use one horn as a tent weight and make no secret of it.

## Season and living things

| Season | Work, passage and risk |
|---|---|
| Thaw | The Lime ford goes under brown water. The bridge can carry people while wagons queue for inspection. Orc outriders inspect Ram Saddle before families move. Snow still covers the east lip after the west looks green. |
| High summer | Both hill routes open. Shepherds move onto the benches. A pair of wyverns breeds above White Scar on the lodge trail. Fresh sheep bones accumulate beneath the nest. A guide can use the lower spring path, adding four hours each way. The pair hunt farther when the lamb flocks descend. |
| Harvest | Seed Shelf cuts and dries hay before oats are carted to the lodge. Keld buys fodder before it commits to winter patrols. Red Well's households move east before snow closes the saddle. Reed Tower's human tenants harvest even when the garrison has orders to withdraw. |
| Winter | Budget 120 days. The saddle closes to routine traffic. Between Lime End and the lodge the bench trail remains intermittent. Ten usable delivery windows are the ordinary-year allowance. A missed window consumes stores. River ice can stop a barge well before it bears a wagon. |

Ravens follow the north-road columns. Foxes raid lodge middens. Above Red Well a hill giant called Old Senn keeps sheep and two white dogs. Orc households pay him his salt in autumn for guarding the eastern strays. He keeps any unbranded lamb born in his fold. Nobody at Gannet knows his name. A soldier asking who owns the heights will be told they are empty. The ravens, wyverns and giant can all be present without a single corpse rising. There is no Royal Seal in this district and the King has never visited it.

## Authority, hatred and who pays for it

**Captain Hester Vann**, Vardanian commander at Gannet, has forty crown soldiers on her roll, thirty-one fit for duty. Her district warrant covers the bridge and military road up to Reed. She can halt traffic for inspection, call in the patrol and requisition cartage against a signed claim. She cannot surrender the Kharadi right of return. She lost two brothers when the causeways fell. Her mother now lets beds to fugitives at three times the old rate. They eat together every seventh day.

**Master Dorr Kett**, Keld's road mason, can condemn a span under his own mark. **Captain Vara Flint**, commander of the returning shield company, can order her own dwarves to march or wait. They are cousins and detest one another. Vara blames human cowardice for every retreat since Dunmere. When a wounded human said her company had left him at Reed, she called him a liar in front of the stretcher crews. He was telling the truth. She gave the order because her own wounded filled the wagons.

**Chief Gurra Split-Tusk** commands the Red Well clan. Her elder brother raided the Lime End steads six years ago, drove off the cattle and hanged a farmer from his own hay crane. Gurra rode with him. She still wears the farmer's silver buckle. Her niece now sells remounts to the queen. At Gannet every orc must find a resident surety before the watch will admit them after dark. Human strangers can pay a penny and enter. Vann tolerates this rule. The ostler rents his signature to orcs who can pay. An orc carter born at Lime End slept outside the walls all last winter. Her taxes were collected on time.

**Bailiff Oren Vale** assigns town billets and holds the common barn keys. His lists describe halfling households sheltering kin as having “spare beds” wherever two children share one. His own widowed sister occupies four rooms over the weighhouse. The halfling moot has begun laying out a place at supper for every absentee charged to its account. The bowls down the middle of the table are a better billet register than Vale's book. The moot can deny its own carts to private buyers. It cannot countermand a crown warrant.

**Iron-rank Nella Pike** leads Crow House's remaining field team. The house owes a response to any rescue bell. She chooses the route and who goes. Last winter a donor demanded that she leave an orc boy on the hill and bring his shepherd down first. She brought down the boy, whose hip was broken. The shepherd walked. The donor took back his two mules. This winter the house pays freight. Nobody has found another donor.

**Eda Rusk**, keeper at Crow Lodge, assigns beds from her own lease and can shelter whoever fits. She cannot promise grain pledged to the twelve winter shares. **The Red Well hearth heads** can offer a stranger a night's shelter and water. Long grazing needs Gurra's word. **Dorr** can admit an inspection party to the road galleries. Keld's gate captain grants entry to the inhabited hold. A royal seal on a Vardanian letter gets its bearer a hearing at the outer gate.

## Three checks against the map

1. **Grain in an ordinary winter and a bad one.** Twelve people at 0.5 kg a day for 120 days need 720 kg. Seed Shelf's autumn carts put 480 kg in the lodge, ten winter packs carry 30 kg grain each alongside other goods, and the closing reserve is 60 kg. This assumes the economic reference's two-mule, four-day round trip from Lime End, with winter water at the sheltered bench spring. In the bad-year case deduct the 120 kg diverted at Gannet from the 480 kg autumn allocation (the diverted grain went by pack from Seed Shelf to Lime End and by cart from there to Gannet). With four winter trips lost, 360 + 180 = 540 kg. The deficit is 180 kg, thirty days' grain for the lodge. The missing two sacks would cover twenty days if returned. The last ten days would still have to come from another store. The bad-year weather is a stress test. In the year-eight post-harvest account the diversion leaves 360 kg at the opening of winter. Before harvest these are allocations against a standing crop. Neither figure fixes the season of play.
2. **A company comes home.** Vara brings forty-eight dwarves from Reed, six of them unable to walk, on two casualty wagons with their drivers among the forty-eight. Day one gets them to Gannet. Day two takes them to Lime End. Days three and four take them up the maintained high road to Keld. Assigning 1 kg of travelling food per person per day costs 192 kg over four days, separately from fodder and treatment. A day held at the bridge adds 48 kg and another day's draught feed. The saddle route cannot take either wagon. Dorr can certify a damaged arch and stagger loads. Vann's demand for the wounded to be handed over as the price of passage would violate the treaty.
3. **The bell rings above the lodge.** A herder falls halfway from Crow Lodge to Ram Saddle. His sister walks down in about two hours and rings the lodge bell at noon. Pike has four bearers and two relief hands on site in this test. The ascent takes about 2½ hours, assessment and rigging one hour, the loaded descent five hours. They reach Eda about 8:30 p.m. in summer light or with lamps near the end. White Scar lies on the Lime End leg and adds no time to this recovery. Taking the casualty onward to Lime End costs two long carrying days plus rest as needed. A messenger can leave first. A team summoned from Gannet could never have reached the casualty that afternoon.

The tests are regional planning cases. The opening took place in a remote forested valley. Its position relative to the lodge, road, grasslands and lake approaches remains unknown.
