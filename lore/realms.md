# Realms and old quarrels

A traveller following the water south through Avarra leaves the ice cap for a belt of northern lakes. Three great rivers drain the lakes through Vardane and into the warmer valleys of Cyranne. Kharad rises to the east of the upper rivers. To their west the Greywood runs along a low watershed. Beyond the eastern mountains lie the high grasslands, where orc clans move their herds between summer ranges and winter valleys. Several spring routes cross the same saddles the dwarves use to reach their western holdings.

## The river crowns

Queen Mara IV of Vardane holds court in the south of her own kingdom after two retreats. Northern castles originally controlled the roads between the lakes. Many survive on islands or heights behind the main advance. A castle flying Mara's colours may command a stretch of water and have no overland contact with the next loyal garrison.

King Edren II of Cyranne has sent his northern regiments across the border. His estates grant extraordinary levies for six months at a time. Both crowns remember the succession war in which a Cyrannese prince used an invitation to lead Vardane's army as grounds to claim its throne. Mara gives Edren's commanders particular districts and duties in writing. She keeps command of her royal host.

## The eastern passes and the Greywood

The Holds of Kharad govern themselves under their own sovereigns. Their assembly directs the high roads that join them. The old treaty with Vardane grants passage and quarters to dwarven companies coming west to relieve the frontier. It also requires Vardane to keep the western bridgeheads open for their return. Closing one behind an allied army would breach an oath sworn in both kingdoms.

The Greywood court once lent Vardane spearmen during a succession struggle. In return the crown reserved the western bank of Alder Brook to the elves. The brook changed course during a flood. Human grants now cover the strip between the old channel and the new one. The elven signatory is alive and can show a visitor the trees the treaty witnesses blazed. Three generations of human families have graves in the disputed strip.

## Beyond the front

The Serevan League holds ports on both sides of the southern straits. Its cities maintain a common convoy fleet while retaining their own councils. Beyond the southern shore lies Mereth, where Amareth commands the inland river basin. Its sovereign controls the levy and its canal councils determine when the fields can release their water. Neither the League nor the Avarran crowns can command Amareth's ships.

Tirren lies weeks west by sea. Its Talren Compact binds the eastern ports and several river peoples to mutual defence. A coastal signatory can grant a fleet anchorage. Only the upriver communities can grant land to settlers above the tidewater. Avarran envoys have already discovered how much turns on the wording of an invitation.
