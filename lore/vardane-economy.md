# The economy of Vardane
Campaign reference • Eighth year of the invasion • Southern provisioning market and wilderness routes

## Coins and wages

A Silver Mark changes for 16 Copper Pennies. A Gold Hart changes for 20 Marks or 320 Pennies. In the historical comparisons below, one English penny (d) maps to four Vardanian Pennies, a shilling to three Marks and a pound sterling to three Harts. This is a purchasing-power convention. It establishes neither bullion weights nor an exchange rate imposed on foreign crowns.

Use 1 Mark for a paid day of ordinary building labour and 1½ Marks for a master building craftsman in a functioning southern market. Munro's southeastern English series for 1436–40 gives 4d and 6d respectively. Colne Priory's actual 1440 payments include 4d per day each for a roofer and a harness repairer. Individual bargains can differ from the regional average. A day rate applies to days hired. [Wage series](https://memdb.libraries.rutgers.edu/sites/default/files/munro/WagesPricesAntwerpEngl.pdf) · [Colne account](https://wwwe.lib.cam.ac.uk/earls_colne/account2/45200242.htm)

## Farm, mill and market

The southern grain benchmark is 332 Pennies per quarter of wheat (eight bushels), based on the 1431–40 mean of 6s 11d. The preceding decade yields 259 Pennies. Wool moved the other way, from 358 to 276 Pennies per 28 lb tod. Keep grain volume and mass distinct. For working Vardanian wheat accounts adopt a standard dry bushel weighing 24 kg at reference condition. Actual damp or light grain is weighed. This local convention yields 192 kg per quarter and approximately 1.73 Pennies per kg in bulk. It does not apply to oats or hay. [Bradley, chapter VI, table I](https://readingroo.ms/2/9/2/5/29258/29258-h/29258-h.htm)

The Alciston barley account for 1378 returned 3.75 volumes harvested for each sown. A farm using that scenario and sowing 40 measures gathers 150, reserves 40 to sow again and has 110 before dues, losses, feed and household consumption. At a threefold yield that balance falls to 80. Enter the farm's actual lease separately. A money rent still falls due after a poor harvest. A share rent falls with output. In charter towns a small wheat loaf can retain its Copper Penny price while the baker changes the posted loaf weight with the price of flour. The assize supplies the practice. Local milling, fuel and grade determine the weight. [Alciston account explained](https://www.bahs.org.uk/crop-yields-database/the-data/) · [Assize of Bread](https://sourcebooks.web.fordham.edu/source/breadbeer.asp)

## Housing and a household that has to balance

Two Colne holdings illustrate the spread even within one rental. A cottage and garden owed 6d a year (24 Pennies). A tenement with cottage and adjoining croft owed 2s 8d (8 Marks) at four terms. These are payments attached to specified holdings. Someone arriving with cash has no automatic right to take over such a tenancy. Use the first for an established cottage holding and the second for a larger customary holding. For a new southern town tenancy, adopt a campaign contract of 2 Marks per month for a single unfurnished room with common hearth, payable monthly. This market lease is a design assumption. Twelve months cost 24 ordinary paid days. [Cottage and garden](https://wwwe.lib.cam.ac.uk/earls_colne/survey2/43000186.htm) · [Cottage and croft](https://wwwe.lib.cam.ac.uk/earls_colne/survey2/43000846.htm)

Worked annual cash account for two adults and one child, renting that room with no land. Count the child as half an adult for this example. For the grain, pulses, meat and fat floor we use Allen's comparison basket, scaled to 2½ adult equivalents. This is a low-consumption household. The proposed Vardanian unit prices other than wheat are model inputs for ordinary southern purchases. They can be replaced individually. At 1 Mark per paid day the household needs 178 paid days across its earners. At 210 paid days it takes in 3,360 Pennies, leaving 512 Pennies or 32 Marks for savings and exceptional expenses. At 160 days the shortfall is 288 Pennies or 18 Marks. If retail grain rises from 2 to 3 Pennies/kg, the same family needs 425 additional Pennies, or 27 more whole paid days. Its 210-day surplus shrinks to 87 Pennies. If heating and lighting also double it runs a 233-Penny deficit. These are alternative employment and price conditions. Neither a full working year nor survival on a northern winter fuel allowance is presumed.

## Equipment, animals and finished boats

At the southern market use 4 Pennies per simple halter (three purchased together), 1 Mark 8 Pennies for a horse rope, 2 Marks for an ordinary axe, 2 Marks 8 Pennies to 3 Marks 8 Pennies for recorded collar repairs and 4 Harts for a cart horse. Two iron cart wheels cost 3 Harts 10 Marks 8 Pennies in the recorded transaction. A complete working cart horse and cart appears in a mixed agreement involving iron goods at 50s 8d. Keep its full terms attached when comparing it with a cash sale. [Colne Priory account, 1440](https://wwwe.lib.cam.ac.uk/earls_colne/account2/45200242.htm)

A guild that charters a grain boat pays for the actual voyage. A shipyard replacing the vessel needs the capital price. A wreck bought for timber cannot serve as a cheap functioning equivalent. For lock charges use a local charter where established. A useful precedent is Reading's 1405 agreement specifying 1d for a burgess's shout and 2d for a stranger's shout, equivalent to 4 and 8 Pennies per passage. ## Pack routes, winter stores and an actual wilderness price

For planning adopt a sound 450 kg mule and a conservative **90 kg total carried load**, including tack. Allow 15 kg for saddle and panniers, leaving 75 kg before supplies. These are campaign working limits. The military pack manual discusses loads near 20% of body weight. Military transport ration tables give 14 lb hay and 10 lb oats per mule-day. Round this to 11 kg combined daily carried feed for a working trip without grazing. The packer's own carried ration is a campaign allowance of 1 kg per day. Water must be available on the route. [Pack Animal Operations](https://thepeakinc.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/07/FM31-27.-Pack-animal-Operations.-pdf.pdf) · [Transport reference, pack-mule rations](https://www.marines.mil/Portals/1/Publications/FM%2055-15.pdf)

For wintering stock at rest, Maryland Extension's starting estimate is 2% of body weight daily in hay. For a 450 kg horse that is 9 kg/day. Over 120 days it consumes 1,080 kg. If 10% of stored hay is lost to handling and spoilage, put up **1,200 kg per animal**. For four animals, reserve 4.8 tonnes. The 120-day season and loss fraction are campaign assumptions. At a southern hay contract of 1 Penny/kg delivered into the loft, one animal's winter hay costs 75 Marks. Cold, work, feed quality and a longer winter change the reserve. A guild cannot use the same stack both to overwinter its own animals and provision passing packs. [University of Maryland winter hay calculation](https://extension.umd.edu/resource/calculating-your-horses-winter-hay-needs)

Worked winter route. One handler and two mules serve a lodge two days from a valley depot. The four-day round trip has reliable water, no grazing and no return cargo. The animals carry 180 kg total. Subtract 30 kg tack, 88 kg animal feed, 4 kg human food and 8 kg shelter and tools. **50 kg remains for outward goods.** For this contract charge 4 Marks for the handler, 44 kg hay at 1 Penny/kg and 44 kg concentrate at 2 Pennies/kg (a local mixed ration for costing), 16 Pennies for the handler's food and 28 Pennies to hire both animals and tack for the trip. Total **240 Pennies**, or **4.8 Pennies per delivered kg**. The hire is an agreed short-term price for animals whose owner has winter feed, rather than provision for their whole upkeep. At this tariff each animal earns 14 Pennies per trip towards ownership costs. Even nine trips over winter yield only 126 Pennies each against 1,200 Pennies of hay at rest. Such a hire price works as supplementary income for a farm already keeping the stock for other work. A dedicated winter carrier must recover upkeep elsewhere or charge more.

For a carrier maintained solely for that route, allocate the 120-day winter across ten four-day trips and eighty idle days. Charge the eighty idle days at 9 kg hay per mule per day, grossed up for 10% loss: 1,600 Pennies for both animals or 160 Pennies per trip. The trip feed is already in the 240-Penny account. Add the idle feed allocation to reach **400 Pennies per trip**, or **8 Pennies/kg**. This includes the original 28-Penny hire allowance as a contribution to tack, capital and repairs. All quotations below use that dedicated-carrier rate and local stall markups rounded to whole coins.

Check a manifest: 30 kg wheat, 14 kg salt and four axes fill 50 kg. Acquisition costs 244 Pennies, freight 400 and receipts 718. The shop earns 74 Pennies gross before rent, loss or unsold stock. A 120-day winter with ten trips averages one trip every twelve days. Even dedicating every trip to grain brings only 500 kg all winter. For twelve residents consuming 0.5 kg grain each per day, winter demand is 720 kg. They need at least 220 kg in store before winter even in that all-grain case. With the mixed manifest repeated ten times, required opening grain rises to 420 kg. The 0.5 kg is a settlement provisioning assumption. This lodge brings its staple in by autumn wagon and reserves winter carriage for shortages and goods worth the freight.

A six-day outward trip with the same two animals and no grazing, caching or purchased feed requires 264 kg of animal feed alone for the round trip. The animals' entire gross capacity is 180 kg. The winter service ends at the last stocked relay. Beyond it the lodge sells existing inventory until depleted. A higher price cannot create a delivery. An established east-pass cache changes the route only when its stocks and custodian are accounted for.

## The war and regional use

Vardane's south supplies the river market. A cut bridgehead can strand grain and produce shortages downstream while the upstream seller loses customers. The eastern holds depend on treaty passage back across Vardane's bridgeheads. Mountain traffic competes with seasonal orc herds for passes and forage. Charge for grazing only where a clan or other landholder actually grants it. The Greywood's disputed bank makes access to fuel and timber a question of rights as well as labour. The Serevan convoy fleet can carry grain from Mereth across the straits, but Amareth's sovereign and canal councils govern different parts of the decision to release it. Tirren lies weeks away by sea. Its ports can offer anchorage while lacking title to inland farms. These differences govern who can sell, grant passage or refuse export. A single war surcharge would hide them. [Realms and treaties](https://github.com/NoxAnimusVicta/Fantasy-Stories/blob/main/lore/realms.md)

For a guild rescue the family pays zero. On the two-mule winter route the guild must still cover carriage or arrange a subsidy. A crown requisition transfers real stores out of the local account when collected. A promise of reimbursement is a receivable until redeemed. Track grain, transport capacity and cash separately. A town may have coin to pay its carriers and no permitted crossing.

## Scope and continuity

This is an economic reference for the campaign during worldbuilding. The opening is in the wilderness. No protagonist, starting purchases, fictional date of play or scene is established here. Prices explicitly designated as local contracts and model allowances are original campaign decisions. Historical observations retain their units, location and transaction type. For a new quote, record quantity, quality, locality, season, terms and whether carriage is already included. Use the nearest comparable transaction and alter the actual changed costs. Apply a route charge only once.
