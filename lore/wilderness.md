# Monsters, wilderness and adventurers

The oldest rescue bells hung beside roads where a person could disappear half a day's walk from home. Charcoal burners, shepherds and quarrymen worked farther from the gates than a town watch could patrol. Adventuring houses kept teams who could follow somebody into that country, spend the night and bring them home. Experienced guides still know districts that have never appeared on a royal map.

## Creatures of Avarra

| Creature and range | How people recognise its presence | Why someone goes looking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wyverns of the Kharadi escarpment | A breeding pair takes sheep from the high terraces and leaves bones on ledges below its eyrie. Its young can fly well before they can hunt. | A herd can shift downhill for a season. A quarry, beacon or mountain road must stay where it is. Houses take contracts to locate the eyrie and establish which route can still be used. |
| Barrow trolls of the western fens | Earth caves beneath old embankments. A troll swims well, spends daylight in a dry hollow and returns to the same crossing when cattle come down to drink. | A missing drover may have reached a patch of high ground. Finding him before the water rises can matter more than collecting the bounty on the troll. |
| Web spiders of the Greywood fringe | A woodcutter finds a narrow game trail suddenly roofed over in grey silk. Small birds cease calling close to a nest. | Experienced woodcutters abandon a stand. Strangers cutting timber under a disputed grant may carry on until a whole working party goes missing. |
| Hill giants of the eastern grasslands | Old stone sheepfolds above the tree line are built to a stride several times an orc's. Occupied folds have smoke fires and dogs. | Giants speak, keep herds and remember agreements. A clan may hire an interpreter to recover stolen stock or a veteran team to hunt a giant who has begun carrying off herders. |
| Dragons of the high peaks | A single dragon can occupy a ruined hold for centuries. Neighbouring clans date grazing rights and abandoned roads to its last waking. | A dragon stirring brings royal envoys, would-be slayers and descendants of the dispossessed. A guild can make a name by getting the first survey party back alive. |

## The houses at work

A Bronze adventurer can undertake an ordinary local search. An unfamiliar range, a vanished patrol or signs of several predators can put a team under an Iron leader. A rescue bell commits the house to answer. The guildmaster chooses whom to send and can call on another house if the return journey will need more hands. The full rank table lives in [The living world](living-world.md).

The war has drawn veteran teams north and left established routes untended. Abandoned winter pastures give wyverns new nesting ground. A deserted fort can become a troll den even where the King has never set foot. Farther south the old contracts continue. A father ringing the bell because his child failed to come home at dusk has no need to know which army holds Dunmere.
